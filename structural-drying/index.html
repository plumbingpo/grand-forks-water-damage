Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Grand Forks Restoration know it well. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Common Structural Drying Questions

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days. Factors affecting the timeline include the extent of saturation, types of materials affected, ambient conditions and building construction. Commercial projects or heavy saturation may take 5 to 7 days or more. Daily monitoring lets us keep you informed about the timeline.
We use calibrated moisture meters to measure moisture content in all affected materials. We compare the numbers against reference materials from areas that stayed dry and against the ranges IICRC S500 accepts, and only then call it complete. We never rely on touch or appearance alone.
It is our documented set of readings, kept for the whole drying process. You can see progressive moisture reduction for every material that was wet. Final readings are documented when materials reach dry standard. The complete log is available for your insurance claim and your own records.
Staying in the house through the drying process is possible in the majority of cases. Noise is the main drawback, since the equipment itself is safe. To make it easier, we can talk through options like adjusting airflow or placing equipment farther from where you sleep. Please leave the equipment running while you are home.
Psychrometry is the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. With it, our IICRC-certified team works out the airflow, the temperature and where each machine should sit. Drywall, hardwood, concrete and plaster each dry at their own pace. Under-drying leaves moisture for mold, and over-drying can harm wood, so the calculations matter.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Grand Forks

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak

Structural Drying, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose Grand Forks Restoration:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Commercial LGR dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers are strategically positioned based on our moisture map. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
