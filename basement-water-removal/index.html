Walking downstairs into standing water is about as stressful as home ownership gets. Heavy rain that overloads the drainage, a dead sump pump, a failed water heater, a broken pipe and foundation seepage all put water downstairs, where it has to be dealt with by professionals right away before it damages the structure, grows mold and destroys what you store there. Homeowners across Grand Forks can reach Grand Forks Restoration 24/7 for emergency water removal, and our crews bring heavy extraction gear and advanced dryers so the basement is safe and dry again without delay.

Drying a basement is its own challenge: the space is below ground level, air barely circulates and dampness lingers. Between poured concrete underfoot, concrete block on every side and wet soil outside, water has nowhere to go and evaporation on its own is slow. To handle that, our crew uses equipment made for the job, including submersible pumps where the water is deep, truck-mounted units when there is a lot of it, and carefully placed dehumidifiers with air movers built for drying below ground.

Beyond removing the water, we address the root cause of your basement flooding to help keep it from happening again. To find contributing factors, we check grading, window wells, the sump pump, penetrations through the walls and the drainage at the foundation. We then recommend long-term solutions such as a backup sump pump, an interior drainage system or waterproofing measures that protect your basement from future water intrusion.

Pumping the water out is usually a 4 to 8 hour job. Complete structural drying of a basement usually takes 4 to 7 days, because concrete holds moisture and the space sits below grade. Our crew checks moisture each day and pulls the dryers only once the readings show the structure is properly dry.

Warning Signs for Basement Water Removal

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water seeping through floor cracks, wall joints or around pipe penetrations
  • Stained or deteriorating carpet, tile or other basement flooring
  • Leaks at the window frames below grade, or wells holding water outside them
  • A sump pump that runs nonstop, does not run at all or makes unusual noises
  • Water visibly pooled on the floor downstairs, no matter how it got there
  • Powdery white mineral buildup, called efflorescence, across block or concrete walls
  • Drywall gone soft and paint flaking off in a finished basement

Basement Water Removal Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Extend each downspout so the water lands 6 feet or more from the foundation
  • Check that the ground next to the house still slopes away, and regrade where it has settled
  • Seal visible cracks in basement walls and floors with hydraulic cement
  • Clean leaves and debris out of window wells, and think about fitting covers over them
  • Add battery backup to your sump system so it keeps working when the power fails
  • Clogged gutters dump water at the base of the house, so clear them out each season

Why Professional Basement Water Removal Matters

Removing water from a basement calls for know-how and equipment well past the consumer fans and pumps most homeowners own. Our submersible pumps move water much faster than anything you can rent, while commercial dryers control humidity, airflow and temperature, the conditions a below-grade structure needs because it resists evaporation on its own. Skip that step and the dampness remaining in insulation, concrete and wood framing will bring mold and break the materials down.

Safety is another concern, because a flooded basement holds dangers other rooms do not. Electrocution is possible whenever water reaches or covers outlets, wiring, the panel or plugged-in appliances. Flooding can also compromise the furnace, the water heater and natural gas lines. We are trained to size up and control each hazard first, then extract, so that everyone in the home and on our crew stays safe during restoration.
