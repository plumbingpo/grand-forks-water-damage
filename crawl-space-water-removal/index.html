Crawl Space Water Removal in Grand Forks

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.
