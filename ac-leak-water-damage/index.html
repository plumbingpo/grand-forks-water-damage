Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Grand Forks

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

The Platform and Closet Walls

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Drywall or insulation that is saturated or moldy is cut out so the cavity behind it can air out.

3

Dry the Structure

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
